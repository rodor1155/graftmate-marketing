# CIS Invoice Example: What to Include and How Deductions Work

A CIS invoice is a normal UK invoice with two extras: labour and materials shown on separate lines, and the CIS deduction shown so the amount due matches what the contractor will actually pay. For example, a registered subcontractor bills £1,800 labour and £650 materials. The contractor deducts 20% of the labour only (£360) and pays £2,090. Materials you paid for directly and VAT are left out of the deduction. If you and the contractor are both VAT registered, the invoice will usually also need a domestic reverse charge note, and you charge no VAT. The full worked example is below, with each rule linked to its gov.uk source.

## What is a CIS invoice?

Under the Construction Industry Scheme (CIS), a contractor takes money off a subcontractor's payment and passes it to HMRC. HMRC describes these deductions as [advance payments towards your tax](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-subcontractor/pay-tax-and-claim-back-deductions).

There is no official HMRC "CIS invoice" form. The contractor is the one who works out and makes the deduction. Your job is to send an invoice clear enough that they can work it out correctly, which means splitting labour from materials. If you send a single lump sum, the contractor has nothing to show which part is materials, and you risk the deduction being taken off the lot.

### The three CIS deduction rates

The rate depends on your status with HMRC, as set out in [HMRC's guidance for contractors](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/make-deductions-and-pay-subcontractors):

| Your status | Deduction rate |
|---|---|
| Registered for CIS as a subcontractor | 20% |
| Not registered for CIS | 30% |
| Gross payment status | 0% (no deduction) |

The contractor is given the rate by HMRC when they verify you. If you are not registered, [contractors must deduct 30%](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-subcontractor/how-to-get-paid), so registering is worth sorting before your first invoice.

## What to include on a CIS invoice

### The basics every UK invoice needs

Gov.uk lists [what an invoice must include](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers/invoices-what-they-must-include):

- a unique identification number
- your company name, address and contact information
- the company name and address of the customer you are invoicing
- a clear description of what you are charging for
- the date the goods or service were provided (the supply date)
- the date of the invoice
- the amount or amounts being charged
- the VAT amount, if applicable
- the total amount owed

Sole traders must also show their own name and any business name they trade under. Limited companies must show the full company name as it appears on the certificate of incorporation.

If you are VAT registered, the invoice also has to [show your VAT number and display the VAT separately](https://www.gov.uk/vat-record-keeping/vat-invoices).

### The CIS extras

These are not on the gov.uk invoice list, but they are what makes the invoice workable under CIS:

- **Labour and materials on separate lines**, each with its own subtotal.
- **Your UTR** (Unique Taxpayer Reference), so the contractor can match you to their CIS records.
- **The CIS deduction line**, showing the rate and the amount.
- **The net amount payable** after the deduction, so nobody is surprised by the bank transfer.
- **The reverse charge note**, if it applies (covered below).

## How CIS deductions work: labour vs materials

The deduction is not taken off the whole invoice. HMRC tells contractors to [start with the total (gross) amount of the subcontractor's invoice and then take away](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/make-deductions-and-pay-subcontractors) the amount the subcontractor paid for:

- VAT
- equipment which is now unusable ("consumable stores")
- fuel used, except for travelling
- equipment hired for the job ("plant hire")
- manufacturing or prefabricating materials
- materials, but only if the subcontractor paid for them directly

The CIS percentage is then deducted from whatever is left. In practice, that is your labour.

Two points catch people out:

1. **Materials only count if you paid for them directly.** If the contractor supplied the materials, there is nothing to take off.
2. **Travel fuel is not excluded.** Fuel used on the job comes off before the deduction, but fuel for travelling does not.

Keep your supplier receipts. If the contractor queries the materials figure, you will want to be able to back it up.

## Worked CIS invoice example

Here is a full example. Dave Harris is a sole trader plasterer, registered for CIS at 20% and VAT registered. He has done a week's work for a VAT-registered main contractor, who is selling the work on to their own client and so is not an end user. The reverse charge applies.

> **INVOICE**
>
> **From:** Dave Harris t/a Harris Plastering, 14 Mill Lane, Leeds LS12 4AB
> Tel: 0113 496 0123 | UTR: 12345 67890 | VAT no: GB 123 4567 89
>
> **To:** Northfield Construction Ltd, Unit 3, Canal Works, Leeds LS9 8CD
>
> **Invoice number:** 0147
> **Invoice date:** 12 October 2026
> **Date of supply:** 5 to 9 October 2026
> **Job:** Skim and finish to first floor, Plot 6, Canal Works
>
> | Description | Amount |
> |---|---|
> | Labour: 5 days plastering at £360 per day | £1,800.00 |
> | Materials: plaster, beads, scrim, PVA (paid for by subcontractor) | £650.00 |
> | **Net total** | **£2,450.00** |
> | VAT at 20% (£490.00): reverse charge, customer to pay the VAT to HMRC | £0.00 |
> | **Invoice total** | **£2,450.00** |
> | Less CIS deduction: 20% of labour (£1,800.00) | −£360.00 |
> | **Amount payable** | **£2,090.00** |
>
> *Reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies. Customer to pay the VAT to HMRC.*
>
> Payment terms: 14 days. Bank details: Sort code 00-00-00, Account 00000000.

All names, numbers and details in this example are made up.

### How the numbers were worked out

1. Labour £1,800 + materials £650 = **£2,450** net.
2. No VAT is charged because the reverse charge applies. The £490 is shown so the contractor knows what to account for.
3. The contractor takes the invoice total and removes the materials: £2,450 − £650 = **£1,800**.
4. CIS at 20% of £1,800 = **£360**.
5. Dave is paid £2,450 − £360 = **£2,090**. The contractor pays the £360 to HMRC.

### The same job in other situations

| Situation | VAT charged | CIS deduction | Paid to subcontractor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registered (20%), reverse charge applies | £0 | £360 | £2,090 |
| Registered (20%), not VAT registered | £0 | £360 | £2,090 |
| Registered (20%), normal VAT (customer is an end user) | £490 | £360 | £2,580 |
| Not registered for CIS (30%), not VAT registered | £0 | £540 | £1,910 |
| Gross payment status, reverse charge applies | £0 | £0 | £2,450 |

In the normal VAT row, the deduction is still £360. VAT is taken out before the CIS percentage is applied, so charging VAT never increases your deduction.

## The reverse charge VAT note

The [VAT domestic reverse charge for building and construction services](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-domestic-reverse-charge-for-building-and-construction-services) has applied since 1 March 2021. Where it applies, you do not charge VAT. Your customer accounts for it to HMRC instead.

### When it applies

According to [HMRC's technical guide](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-reverse-charge-technical-guide), it broadly applies when:

- you and your customer are both VAT registered in the UK
- the work is reported within CIS
- the supply is standard or reduced rated (it does not apply to zero-rated work)
- your customer is not an end user or intermediary supplier

An end user is a VAT and CIS registered business that does not sell the construction services on. If a customer tells you in writing that they are an end user, normal VAT rules apply and you charge VAT as usual. HMRC says you should always ask the customer whether they are registered for VAT and CIS, and whether or not they are an end user.

If you are not VAT registered, none of this applies to your invoices.

### What to write on the invoice

HMRC says the invoice must show all the usual VAT invoice information, plus a note making clear that the reverse charge applies and that the customer must account for the VAT. It should state how much VAT is due under the reverse charge, or the rate if the amount cannot be shown. The VAT must not be included in the amount charged.

HMRC gives these [examples of wording that meet the legal requirement](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-reverse-charge-technical-guide):

- "Reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies"
- "Reverse charge: S55A VATA 94 applies"
- "Reverse charge: Customer to pay the VAT to HMRC"

### Reverse charge and materials

CIS and the reverse charge treat materials differently:

- **CIS:** the deduction is on labour only. Materials you paid for are excluded.
- **Reverse charge:** where materials are supplied along with the construction service, HMRC says the reverse charge applies to the full value of the invoice, materials included.

That is why the example shows £490 of reverse charge VAT (20% of the full £2,450) but only £360 of CIS (20% of the £1,800 labour).

## After you send the invoice

If the contractor makes a deduction, they must give you a [payment and deduction statement within 14 days of the end of each tax month](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-contractor/make-deductions-and-pay-subcontractors). HMRC's own example is a tax month ending 5 June, where the statement is due by 19 June.

Keep every statement, because they are your proof of the tax already paid:

- **Sole traders and partners** [record the full pay before deductions as income](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-subcontractor/pay-tax-and-claim-back-deductions) on the Self Assessment return, and the total deductions as "CIS deductions". HMRC then takes the deductions off the bill.
- **Limited companies** claim deductions back through the company's monthly payroll scheme.

## FAQs

### Is CIS deducted from materials?

No, as long as you paid for the materials directly. HMRC tells contractors to take the cost of materials, VAT, plant hire, consumable stores and non-travel fuel off the invoice total before applying the CIS rate. Show materials on a separate line so the contractor can see the split.

### Do I have to show the CIS deduction on my invoice?

The contractor is responsible for working out and making the deduction, and gov.uk's list of what an invoice must include does not mention CIS. Showing the deduction and the net amount payable is still good practice, because both sides can see the expected payment and mistakes get spotted before the money moves.

### Is the CIS deduction calculated before or after VAT?

Before. VAT is removed from the invoice total before the CIS percentage is applied, so the deduction is based on your net labour figure. Whether you charge VAT normally, use the reverse charge or are not VAT registered, the CIS deduction on the same job is the same.

### What happens if I am not registered for CIS?

Contractors must deduct 30% from your payments instead of 20%. On the worked example above, that is £540 instead of £360. You can find out how to register in [HMRC's guidance for CIS subcontractors](https://www.gov.uk/what-you-must-do-as-a-cis-subcontractor/how-to-get-paid).

### What reverse charge wording should I put on a CIS invoice?

HMRC accepts wording such as "Reverse charge: Customer to pay the VAT to HMRC" or "Reverse charge: VAT Act 1994 Section 55A applies". State the amount of VAT due under the reverse charge (or the rate), and do not add it to the total you are charging.
